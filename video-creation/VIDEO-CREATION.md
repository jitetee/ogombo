# Video creation rules (Ezra Ogombo)

Read this before writing or building any video script, scene list or thumbnail.
It lives outside `public/`, so it is never deployed with the website.
Ka adds to it over time. Newest additions go at the bottom under "Added later".

## 1. House rules (from Ka)

- Every scene has at least 5 drawn items (icons, images, ink marks, emoji, narrator). Plain text does not count toward the 5.
- Long videos run over 10 minutes (about 1,450+ words at 144 words per minute). Aim for 1,700 to 2,000 words.
- Use the site's explainer cards: write and draw on the card images (ring, underline, highlight, handwritten labels) the way the card reels do.
- Thumbnails follow Part 1: cream or white gradient, narrator pointing at the title, red and black marker lettering, red underline, red bar along the bottom, round channel avatar bottom right. Not the teal Episode style.
- Series plan: Part 2 and Part 3 of Surgical Instruments (names and uses) use the 52 instrument cards. A new episode that is not about hand instruments covers theatre equipment.
- Do not mention the TVET PDF. Do not render the old series remake unless asked.
- Audience: students, technicians, clinicians, medics and staff. Retro, plain wording, no filler.

## 2. Script formula: green and purple

Colour-code every script.

- GREEN section = the reason to watch. State a problem, a mystery, a question, or set an expectation.
- PURPLE section = the answer or the lesson.
- Alternate: problem, solution, problem, solution. Question, answer, question, answer.
- Never start teaching straight away. Without a green section the lesson is just information and the viewer has no reason to care. The creator knows why it is valuable; the viewer does not until told.
- Hooks (the opening) are a green section.

### Four ways to create curiosity (green)

1. A mystery, or a problem or question that has no answer yet.
2. Something counterintuitive or surprising.
3. Someone knows a secret, or something important is being hidden.
4. A missing piece: something important the viewer has not yet considered.

### "Intrigue comes from uncertainty"

If the outcome is certain there is no curiosity. Show what could go wrong, what is at stake, what is hard.

### Three-part test for every moment

Before any part stays in, the viewer must be told:
1. What is happening.
2. Why it is important.
3. What could go wrong.

If a part cannot be given curiosity or an expectation, cut it, or summarise it in one or two sentences of voiceover.

### For videos where something is done, not explained

The reason to watch might sit in the destination, but the journey must also earn attention. Add green lines (danger, inexperience, stakes) before each step, even if added later as voiceover.

## 3. Flow: but and therefore, not and then

- Do not chain points with "and then... and then...". It is repetitive and gives the viewer places to leave.
- Link scenes, topics and sentences with "but" and "therefore": "We tried this, but that got in the way. Therefore we did this, but it did not work. So we tried this."
- Every scene should follow from the one before it for a reason the viewer can see.

## 4. Clarity (the biggest retention mistake is confusion)

- The creator is biased: they have all the context. The viewer has none. Re-read every part and ask what a first-time viewer would not understand.
- Tell the viewer what is going on at all times, what is at stake, what could go wrong.
- Being clear and concise is not enough on its own. (Ka's pasted transcript ended here: "...There is three more things to communication." Add the rest under "Added later".)

## 5. Delivery

- Sound like you are sharing something important. Vary volume: louder for excitement, lower for suspense, pause before key points.
- Do not memorise word for word. Mistakes (um, ah) sound more alive than a flat perfect read. Cut them out later.
- Talk to one friend who is interested, not to an audience or a panel of critics.
- The viewer feels what the voice feels.

## 6. Packaging

- Title and thumbnail must make the topic feel interesting to the whole niche, and the intro must make people want the answer.
- The first sentence is a green section. Do not open with the table of contents.

## 7. Checklist before a script is done

- [ ] Opens with a green section using one of the four curiosity methods.
- [ ] Green and purple alternate all the way through.
- [ ] Every scene links to the last with "but" or "therefore".
- [ ] Every scene says what is happening, why it matters, what could go wrong.
- [ ] Parts with no curiosity are cut or shortened to one or two sentences.
- [ ] A first-time viewer could follow every line.
- [ ] Over 10 minutes for long videos.
- [ ] At least 5 drawn items in every scene.
- [ ] Thumbnail follows Part 1 (cream, narrator pointing, red marker lettering).
- [ ] Facts match the card text. Nothing invented.
